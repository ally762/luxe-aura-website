How Much Do Hair Extensions Really Cost? An Honest Breakdown by Method

Luxe Aura Hair·September 28, 2026
  • Hair Extensions
  • Hair Replacement
  • Wigs
Book NowCall Now

"How much do hair extensions cost" is probably the single most-searched question in this entire industry, and it's also one of the most frustrating to research, because most answers online are either wildly vague ("it depends!") or don't reflect what a quality install with real human hair actually requires. So here's our attempt at an honest, specific answer.

Why the Range Is So Wide

Extension pricing depends on four things: the method, the amount of hair used, the quality of the hair itself, and your starting hair condition. A full head of Natural Beaded Rows for someone with fine, damaged hair recovering from chemotherapy is a genuinely different service than a partial K-Tip refresh for someone with thick, healthy hair — and the price should reflect that difference honestly, not hide behind a single flat number.

What Things Typically Start At

These are starting points for a full install using 100% human Slavic hair, not synthetic or lower-grade blends, and they include installation, professional blending, and styling:

●        Tape-In Extensions: starting around $900+ — the fastest full install, ideal if you want volume without heavy daily maintenance.

●        K-Tip (Keratin Bond) Extensions: starting around $900+ — priced similarly to tape-ins, with a longer application process in exchange for more natural individual movement.

●        Natural Beaded Rows (hand-tied wefts): starting around $900+ — our most common recommendation for maximum volume and fullness.

●        Brazilian Knots Extensions: starting around $900+ — a strong, durable option especially suited to curly and textured hair.

●        Non-Surgical Hair Replacement Systems: starting around $900+ — custom-fitted units for more significant thinning or loss.

●        Hair extensions for chemo hair / significant hair loss: starting around $2,500+, which reflects the additional hair volume (up to 150g), coloring, and specialized blending these installs typically require.

What's Actually Included at That Starting Price

A quality quote isn't just for the hair itself. At minimum, it should cover the extension hair, the installation, professional blending into your natural hair, a finishing style, at-home care instructions, and some level of continued support from your stylist afterward. If a quote you're getting elsewhere doesn't include most of those things, ask what's missing — it usually shows up later as a surprise add-on.

What Pushes the Price Above the Starting Point

A few honest factors that move cost upward: more hair volume needed for fuller coverage, additional coloring or lightening to match the extension hair precisely to yours, longer natural hair requiring more extension length, and any pre-treatment your natural hair needs (like straightening) before installation.

Ongoing Costs to Actually Budget For

Extensions aren't a one-time expense — they're a maintenance relationship. Tape-ins typically need to be moved up every 6-8 weeks, K-Tips and microlinks on a similar cycle, and most quality extensions last 3-6 months per set with proper care before needing replacement hair. Budgeting for maintenance appointments, not just the initial install, gives you a much more realistic full picture.

Why "Cheap" Extensions Usually Cost More in the End

Lower-quality synthetic or blended hair tangles faster, loses its shine within weeks, and often needs full replacement far sooner than 100% human hair does. A lower sticker price on the front end frequently means paying for a second full install within months — something worth factoring into any real cost comparison.
